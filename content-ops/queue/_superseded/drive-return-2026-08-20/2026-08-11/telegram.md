*The copy you didn't know you had.*

Most duplicate finders compare filenames, which means they find the copies you'd have spotted anyway and miss the ones you wouldn't.

The Duplicates screen in Drive compares what's actually inside each file. Identical contents land in the same group no matter what the file is called or which folder it ended up in.

The clearest example is on our own drive. One photo of a beach at sunset, three locations. Two of them share a filename, so you'd eventually notice. The third is in the camera roll as `beach-sunset-01.jpg` — same photo, byte for byte, and nothing that searches by name was ever going to connect it to the other two.

Then it stops. Each copy has its own "Keep this" button and nothing moves until you press one. A tool that decides for you is a tool that eventually deletes the copy your accountant had a link to.

One limit worth saying out loud: exact matches only. Export that same photo at a smaller size and it becomes a different file — same picture to you, not to the fingerprint.
