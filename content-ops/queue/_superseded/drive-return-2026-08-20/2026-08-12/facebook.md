You opened a document, changed a few things, saved it. Three weeks later somebody asks what it said before.

Normally that's gone. You either kept a copy called "final v2 OLD" or you didn't.

Your files here keep it for you. Every time you save over something, the version that was there a second ago is kept first — automatically, without you remembering to do anything. That last part is the whole trick: the backup you have to remember to make is the backup you don't have.

Open a file and you see its history. Each version has the date and how big it was, and three buttons: look at it, put it back, or throw it away.

You can also stop and save a version on purpose with a note attached. The document in the picture has one that says "Before the safety review" — so months later nobody has to guess why that version mattered.

It's the undo button, except it survives closing the file, restarting the computer and three weeks going by.

On your own machine, at home.
