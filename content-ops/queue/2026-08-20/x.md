1/
A folder tells you where a file is.

It can't tell you if you already saved it somewhere else, or what it said before you changed it.

Those are the two that actually cost you time.

2/
The same photo, in three places.

Two of them share a filename. The third one is in the camera roll under whatever the camera called it.

It's found by comparing what's inside the file, so a rename can't hide it.

3/
And then it does nothing.

Every copy gets its own "Keep this" button. Nothing moves until you press one.

A deduplicator that decides for you eventually deletes the copy you needed.

4/
The other one is undo, for files.

Save over a document and the version that was there a second ago is kept first, automatically.

Want what it said three weeks ago? One click.

5/
You can also stop and take one on purpose, with a note.

The document in the post has one labelled "Before the safety review".

Automatic snapshots tell you when. A note tells you why.

6/
All of it on your own machine. Nothing goes anywhere.
