*New post: your files, and the two questions a folder can't answer.*

A folder is a good idea. You put something somewhere, and later it's there. What it has never been able to tell you is anything about what's inside — and there are two questions that cost you real time.

**Did I already save this somewhere else?** There's a Duplicates screen that groups files by their contents rather than their names. On this drive it found five groups. The first one is the same photo of a beach in three places, and the third copy is in the camera roll under the name the camera gave it — no amount of looking through folders was going to connect it to the other two. It finds them and stops there: every copy has its own "Keep this" button and nothing moves until you press one.

**What did it look like before I changed it?** Every time you save over a file, the version that was there a second ago is kept first. You don't have to remember to do anything. You can also stop and take one on purpose with a note — the document in the post has one labelled "Before the safety review", which is the difference between knowing *when* something changed and knowing *why*.

Both of them run at home, on your own machine. Nothing about your files goes anywhere.

The post is honest about what isn't there yet: no side-by-side compare between versions, and only exact duplicates are caught — a resized copy of your own photo is invisible to it.
