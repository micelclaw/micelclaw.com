*New post: photograph a receipt, and your inventory fills itself in.*

Everyone who has ever started an inventory has abandoned it in week two. Not because it isn't useful — because keeping it current is a chore that costs you now and pays off later. So the interesting question isn't what the module can store. It's how little you have to do to keep it true.

You photograph a receipt, or press a button on an order confirmation in your mail. A vision model **running on your own machine** reads it and pulls out each product, how many, the unit price, a category, the reference, the shop and the date. Nothing goes to anyone's cloud, because there isn't one.

And then it stops. It leaves you a short queue: Approve, edit, or reject, and you can fix a price before accepting. That's on purpose — a small model reading a crumpled receipt gets a line wrong now and then, and it's better you catch it in two seconds than in six months.

Two things that came out of using it: photograph the same receipt twice and nothing duplicates, and if what you bought looks like electronics, an appliance or a tool, it sets the warranty three years out from the purchase date it read off the paper — then warns you when there are fewer than 90 days left.

The rest is the boring-on-purpose part: one row per thing with what it is, where it is, how many, what it's worth and when the warranty ends. Quantities show as "9 of a minimum 4", and go red when you drop below. And nothing is ever deleted — correct a movement and the original stays, with the correction next to it.

The post has all the screens, and a straight list of what's still missing.
