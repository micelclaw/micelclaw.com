1/ Everyone who has started an inventory has abandoned it in week two.

Not because it isn't useful. Because keeping it current costs you now and pays off later.

So we moved the work off the person.

2/ You photograph a receipt. A vision model reads it and pulls out each product, how many, the unit price, a category, the reference, the shop and the date.

The model runs on your own machine. The photo doesn't go to anyone's cloud, because there isn't one.

3/ Then it stops.

It proposes; it doesn't add. You get a short queue with Approve, an edit pencil and a reject cross, and you can fix a price before you accept it.

4/ That's deliberate. A small model reading a crumpled receipt gets a line wrong now and then.

Better you catch it in the two seconds it takes to glance at a list, than in six months when the numbers don't add up.

5/ Two things that came out of using it:

Photograph the same receipt twice — nothing duplicates.

Buy electronics, an appliance or a tool — it sets your warranty three years out and warns you at 90 days left.

6/ The best inventory feature is the one that means you don't have to do the inventory.
