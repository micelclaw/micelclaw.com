1/ Your money, in three views — and you only ever open the one that's yours.

Personal. Invoicing. Accounting. Same data underneath, three doors in.

2/ Personal answers the question nobody can answer on the spot: am I better off than in January, and where is it going?

Net worth. What came in. What went out. And next to your savings rate as a percentage, the same thing in plain euros.

3/ Invoicing separates four numbers people confuse constantly:

what you've billed · what actually arrived · what's owed and still in date · what's late

The gap between the first two is why businesses that look fine run out of money.

4/ Every headline figure shows its own arithmetic.

The result for the period reads "29.222,33 € invoiced − 7541,98 € expenses". The VAT reads "charged 5113,30 € − paid 1112,48 €".

No black boxes.

5/ Accounting is the lens you open twice a year, when someone asks for something official. It's there so the answer is a click and not an evening.

And a dropdown at the top switches between companies, each with completely separate books.

6/ All of it on your own hardware. Nothing leaves the house.
