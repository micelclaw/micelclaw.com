*New post: your money, in three views — pick the one that's you.*

Most money apps make a promise they can't keep: that one screen works for a person tracking their spending, a freelancer chasing an unpaid invoice, and a company that has to file. You end up with something too heavy for the first and too thin for the last.

So in Micelclaw the first thing you choose isn't a report. It's who you are today.

**Personal** answers the question everyone has and almost nobody can answer on demand: am I better off than I was in January, and what am I spending it on? Net worth, what came in, what went out, your savings rate — and next to the percentage, in plain euros, how much that actually is. Three charts: your net worth over the year, a ring of where the money goes by category, and cash in and out month by month.

**Invoicing** is for whoever sends invoices. Four numbers that are usually confused with each other: what you've billed, what's actually arrived, what's owed to you and still in date, and what's late. Then the same in the other direction for what you owe. How old your unpaid invoices are, and which customers make up most of your business.

**Accounting** is the one you'll open twice a year, and that's fine. It's there so that when your accountant asks for something, the answer is a click.

Underneath all three it's real double-entry bookkeeping, with the Spanish chart of accounts and VAT built in — you just never have to look at it. There's a line in the corner that says "The books balance", and that's the whole interaction you need to have with it.

The post has the screens, and an honest list of what's still missing.
